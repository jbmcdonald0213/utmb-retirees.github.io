Teacher Retirement System of Texas

Where things stand

The 90th Texas Legislature convenes January 12, 2027; bill prefiling opened November 9, 2026. The House and Senate are controlled by different parties this session, which may affect how far any benefit bill gets.

As of the August 31, 2025 valuation, the TRS pension fund had a 35-year funding period, a 77.5% funded ratio, and a $64.9 billion unfunded liability. The liability rose mainly because of the 2025 teacher pay raises (HB 2), which the Legislature did not fund on the pension side. Because the fund is above the 31-year limit, any COLA or 13th check would have to come with new state money to cover its cost. The August 31, 2026 valuation, expected in December 2026, is the key number to watch.

What retiree groups are asking for

The Texas Retired Teachers Association (TRTA)'s 2027 priorities ask the Legislature to (1) bring the TRS funding period back to 31 years or less, and (2) deliver a state-funded benefit increase: a stipend, COLA, or 13th check, with "all options on the table." TRTA says TRS retirees are, on average, about 30% behind inflation. Texas AFT and others continue to push for an automatic COLA tied to inflation.

Social Security

2027 COLA (final)

The Social Security Administration announced a 3.6% increase on October 14, 2026, for nearly 72 million beneficiaries (about 76 million counting SSI). It applies to January 2027 benefits and is the largest increase in four years. The COLA is set by the change in the CPI-W (Consumer Price Index for Urban Wage Earners and Clerical Workers) from the third quarter of one year to the next.

Net effect after Medicare: The Part B premium is rising 3.2% ($6.50/month) to $209.40, which is less than the COLA. That is the first time since 2023 that Part B rose less than the COLA, so most people should keep most of the increase.

For TRS retirees: The Social Security Fairness Act (signed January 2025) repealed the Windfall Elimination Provision (WEP) and Government Pension Offset (GPO). Many TRS retirees who also earned Social Security credits, or who are spouses or survivors of workers, now get larger Social Security checks, and those checks also receive the annual COLA.

Trust fund outlook (2026 Trustees Report, June 9, 2026)

The retirement (OASI) trust fund is projected to run out in late 2032, a year earlier than last year's estimate. The combined retirement and disability funds would last until 2034. If Congress does nothing, incoming taxes would still cover about 78% of scheduled retirement benefits after that date. Solvency legislation could also change how COLAs are calculated, so this is worth watching.

Higher-income retirees pay an extra income-related surcharge (IRMAA) on top of the standard premium: $83.70 to $502.40/month for Part B and $15.40 to $96.40/month for Part D in 2027. IRMAA is based on your tax return from two years earlier, so a one-time income spike (a large IRA withdrawal or a home sale) can raise premiums later. In 2027, negotiated Medicare prices for 15 more high-cost drugs also take effect.

Your retiree health plan works alongside Medicare

UTMB / UT System retirees: Your retiree medical coverage comes from UT Benefits, not TRS-Care. When you become Medicare-eligible you're automatically enrolled in UT CARE Medicare PPO, a group Medicare Advantage plan. You must stay enrolled in Medicare Parts A and B. UT says there are no plan changes to UT CARE for 2027, and retiree-only medical coverage still costs $0 out of pocket. Pre-65 retirees stay on UT SELECT. If you saw news about Blue Cross Blue Shield ending Medicare Advantage plans in 2027, UT has posted a clarification for UT CARE members on its UT CARE page.

For public-school (TRS-Care) retirees, TRS cut TRS-Care Medicare Advantage premiums by 27% for 2027, on top of an approximately 45% cut in 2025. Retiree-only coverage drops from $75 to $50/month, and retiree + spouse from $280 to $230. TRS-Care Standard (for retirees without Medicare) stays at $200/month for retiree-only, unchanged for the tenth year.

Reps. Jayapal, Deluzio, Dingell, Ryan		 Lowers the eligibility age from 65 to 50. Also expands the Medicare Savings Program (help with premiums for lower-income enrollees) and drug-affordability help, and lets people under 400% of the poverty level who don't qualify for that program keep ACA Marketplace subsidies. Most provisions would start six months after enactment and expire after five years unless extended. Introduced 10/22/2026

Sen. Baldwin and others		 Lets Americans ages 50 to 64 buy into Medicare. Introduced Nov. 2026
House Democratic leadership On Nov. 17, 2026, House Democratic Leader Hakeem Jeffries said Democrats should lower the eligibility age and add dental, vision and hearing coverage once they take control of the House in January. He did not name a target age. Statement, no bill yet

Outlook: Neither the House nor the Senate bill has an official cost estimate. The largest age-lowering option the Congressional Budget Office has scored is eligibility at 60, which CBO estimated in 2022 would cost $155 billion through 2031. Any bill would need to pass both chambers and be signed by the president. Sponsors expect House action in 2027.

Why it matters to UTMB retirees: early retirees (under 65) currently rely on UT SELECT or ACA coverage. A lower Medicare age would change how those plans coordinate with Medicare, and could change what retirees and family members under 65 pay.

Other issues affecting Texas retirees

Property taxes

Texas homeowners 65 and older get a $140,000 school-district homestead exemption plus an additional $60,000 ($200,000 total, approved by voters in November 2025), and their school taxes are frozen at the level of the year they first qualified. The freeze can move with you, as a percentage, if you buy a new home. About 60% of Texas homeowners 65 and older now pay no school property tax. County, city and special-district taxes still apply, though many local governments offer their own senior exemptions or freezes (check your appraisal district). Proposals to eliminate school property taxes entirely are expected in the 2027 session.

Federal income taxes

  • Senior deduction: For tax years 2025 through 2028, people 65 and older can take an extra $6,000 deduction ($12,000 for a married couple who are both 65+), which phases out at higher incomes. It reduces taxes on all income, including TRS pensions and Social Security, but it is temporary unless Congress extends it.
  • Texas has no state income tax, so TRS, ORP and Social Security income are taxed only federally.
  • Required minimum distributions from IRAs, 403(b)s and ORP accounts start at age 73 (rising to 75 for people born in 1960 or later).

Other items

  • Optional Retirement Program (ORP): Some UTMB retirees chose ORP, a 401(k)-style plan, instead of the TRS pension. ORP has no COLA; its value depends on investments. TRS COLA bills don't apply to ORP.
  • Working after retirement: TRS has return-to-work limits, and several 2027 bills address them (for example HB 1122). At UT institutions, returning to a benefits-eligible job moves you back to UT SELECT as primary coverage.
  • Medicaid expansion: Bills filed for 2027 would expand Texas Medicaid eligibility. This could matter for lower-income early retirees or family members not yet on Medicare.
  • Social Security Fairness Act: The 2025 repeal of WEP and GPO raised Social Security benefits for many TRS retirees. If you have work history covered by Social Security and haven't checked your benefit, contact SSA.
  • Scams: False "COLA on the ballot" posts have circulated about TRS. TRS and SSA will not call or text asking for your bank or Social Security number.
